# Feature Prioritization

## Features Considered

For the first version of MessMate, I considered the following features:

- Daily meal rating
- Written feedback
- Meal-wise rating
- Weekly food summary
- Favourite dishes
- Meal recommendations
- Mess feedback dashboard
- Notifications

## Prioritization Approach

I would prioritize features based on:

- User value
- Expected impact
- Number of students affected
- Development effort
- Usefulness for mess management

## RICE-Based Prioritization

I used a simple RICE-style approach to compare the features.

| Feature | Reach | Impact | Confidence | Effort |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Daily meal rating | 8 | 8 | 8 | 3 |
| Written feedback | 7 | 7 | 8 | 3 |
| Meal-wise rating | 8 | 8 | 8 | 4 |
| Weekly food summary | 6 | 6 | 7 | 4 |
| Favourite dishes | 5 | 5 | 6 | 3 |
| Meal recommendations | 5 | 6 | 5 | 6 |
| Feedback dashboard | 6 | 7 | 7 | 7 |
| Notifications | 5 | 4 | 6 | 4 |

The numbers above are assumptions for this personal case study and are not based on actual college data.

## MVP Features

Based on the above comparison, I would keep the first version simple and focus on:

1. Today's menu
2. Meal rating
3. Written feedback
4. Basic feedback summary

These features directly support the main problem of collecting and understanding student feedback.

## Future Features

After the basic product is working, features such as:

- Personalised recommendations
- Favourite dishes
- Advanced dashboard
- Notifications

can be explored further.

## Product Decision

My approach is to start with a simple feedback experience instead of building many features at once.

The goal of the MVP is to first understand whether students actually use the product to give regular meal feedback.
